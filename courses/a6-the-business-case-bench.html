Part 1 · covers reading the ladder

Five rungs, one case

5 min live

Each rung changes one thing and leaves the rest alone, so every difference between rungs has a single cause. Read across the ladder, not down a column: the lesson is in how far one change moves the answer.

LiveWhat each rung teaches3 min▶

1 · Base case. NPV +111.7k, IRR 18.4%, but the middle draw is only +20.5k and the chance of loss is 44.1%. The point estimate sits far above the middle because the ranges are skewed against the plan: the build is more likely to overrun than to come in under, and leakage more likely to rise than fall. Analyst session 5 showed why.

2 · The sponsor's case. Saving 16, share 65%, build 330, six years: every value is inside the range Keelson's own teams gave, set at its most hopeful end. NPV +606.4k, IRR 49.1%, chance of loss 15.3%. Nothing was invented. That is why ranges have to be agreed with their owners before anyone sees the result.

3 · Sunk and CDQ charged. NPV -193.3k and a 98.1% chance of loss: the Analyst session 1 errors, both at once. A good project killed by the accounting.

4 · Optimism uplift +25% on build. The build cost raised by a quarter, the correction Leader session 3 discussed. NPV falls to +21.7k and the chance of loss rises to 71.9%. One honest adjustment to an input with one of the smallest tornado bars, but the clearest record of overruns, changes the recommendation.

5 · Every input held certain. The break rung, in Part 2.

Self-studyWhy the rungs change one thing at a time2 min▶

A ladder that changed two things per rung would leave you unable to say which one moved the answer. The same discipline applies to your own case: when a reviewer asks "what if the saving is lower and the build overruns?", run the two as separate rungs first, then together, and report all three. The difference between "together" and the sum of the two separate effects tells you whether the inputs interact; in CFT they do, because the saving and the share multiply each other.

Part 2 · covers the break test

Break it, to prove it measures something

5 min live

A simulation can produce a confident-looking range from almost anything. The test that it measures the inputs and not its own machinery is to remove the uncertainty and check the range disappears. Hold every input at its base value and the bench should give one number, the point estimate, 5,000 times. If it gives anything else, the range was coming from a bug.

LiveThree checks a bench must pass3 min▶

It collapses when the inputs do. Rung 5: every input certain, every draw +111.7k, P10 = P50 = P90. Passed.

It can report a worse number. Rung 3 drives the chance of loss to 98.1%. A tool that can only ever make a case look good is a brochure.

It agrees with an independent build. The engine behind every widget here was written twice, once in the browser and once in Python, and the two agree on every number including all 5,000 draws; a planted error of one part in a thousand is caught. Your own spreadsheet deserves the same test: rebuild the base case NPV by hand once, as in Analyst session 2.

Self-studyWhat the bench still assumes2 min▶

Each input is drawn independently, from a triangle. In reality the share fast-tracked and the leakage probably move together: route more claims past review and more of them will be the doubtful ones. Positive correlation between a benefit and its dis-benefit narrows the range; correlation between two benefits widens it. The triangle is a convention, chosen because owners can give a low, a likely and a high; it has thin tails, so it understates the chance of a very bad outcome. Say both assumptions in the case. The evidence on how far real projects overrun, which argues for fatter tails, is in Leader session 3.

Part 3 · covers the recommendation sentence

From the bench to one sentence

5 min live

The bench produces two dozen numbers. The CFO needs one sentence: what you recommend, the range, how likely a loss is, and the single assumption that decides it, with the person who owns that assumption. Everything else is the appendix.

LiveWhy the recommendation is a judgement, not an output3 min▶

The bench does not say "pilot". At Keelson's 55 percent confidence that the saving comes in high, Analyst session 5 found that piloting costs 8.9 thousand of expected value against committing now, while cutting the worst case from -111.5k to -79.8k. Whether that trade is worth it depends on how much a loss of 111.5 thousand would hurt Keelson this year, which is a question about the company's risk appetite, not about the model. Saying "the model recommends" hides the judgement; saying "we recommend, because a 44.1 percent chance of a loss is more than this budget can carry" owns it.

A team with a stronger balance sheet, or a cheaper pilot, could read the same bench and commit now. Both readings are defensible when they are stated as judgements next to the numbers.

Self-studyThe appendix a reviewer will want2 min▶

Behind the sentence: the cash-flow table with the in and out reasons from Analyst session 1; the discount rate and who set it; the four verdicts side by side; the tornado and the switching-value table; the input ranges with each owner's name; the seed and the number of draws; the ladder; and the stated assumptions (independence, triangles, no terminal value, before tax). A reviewer who can rerun your bench from the appendix will usually trust the sentence. Leader session 4 adds what happens after approval: the benefits register and the dates you will check each assumption against reality.

Homework

After the bench

45 min
  1. Agree your ranges. For the three inputs at the top of your tornado, get the owner to write down a low, a likely and a high before they see any result.
  2. Build your ladder. Base case, the sponsor's case (every input at its hopeful end), one error rung, an uplift rung and a break rung, for your own project.
  3. Write your sentence. Recommendation, range, chance of loss, the deciding assumption and its owner. Read it to someone outside the project.
  4. Hand over to Leader session 4. Turn the deciding assumption into a review date: when will you know its real value, and who will report it?
Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and Analyst session 6 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · The sponsor's case raises NPV from +111.7k to +606.4k. What new facts did it use?

Every value sits inside its agreed range. Choosing where in each range to stand is enough to quintuple the answer, which is why ranges are agreed before results are seen.

2 · With every input held certain, the bench reports P10 = P50 = P90 = +111.7k. What does that prove?

No uncertainty in, no range out. A range that survived the break would be noise from the tool.

3 · Which line belongs in the recommendation sentence?

The sentence carries the decision, the range, the chance of loss and the deciding assumption with an owner. The rest is appendix, where a reviewer can rerun it.
